Current Research

At Synlico, I work on deep reinforcement learning-based causal discovery methods for inferring gene regulatory networks as directed causal graphs from high-dimensional single-cell genomics data.

My research background is in deep learning for graphs. Through the Rensselaer-IBM AI Research Collaboration, I developed graph transformer architectures for molecular representation learning and methods for efficient transformer training.

Selected research

Research Contributions

Work spanning structural graph representations, higher-order molecular geometry, and global attention.

01 Molecular graph learning

Triplet Graph Transformer (TGT)

TGT extends EGT with third-order interactions among groups of three atoms and a 2D-to-3D framework that predicts interatomic distances from 2D molecular graphs for property prediction.

TGT held the highest-ranking single-model position on the PCQM4Mv2 molecular-property prediction benchmark for more than two years and achieved the leading direct-method result on the OpenCatalyst 2020 IS2RE task.

02 Structural representations

Edge-augmented Graph Transformer (EGT)

An early graph transformer architecture that introduced evolving structural edge channels for joint node and pair representations across graph-level prediction, edge classification, and link prediction.

Author and maintainer of the PyTorch and TensorFlow implementations.

03 Efficient attention

Stochastically Subsampled Self-Attention (SSA)

SSA subsamples transformer self-attention during training and formulates the Information Pathways Hypothesis, reducing attention memory and computation by 4–8× across graph, vision, and language tasks.
